# About the historian role

## What the job is

Historians find out about the past from what survives of it and explain
it to others. Occupational data puts the core plainly: gather historical
information from archives, records, images and publications; judge
whether it is genuine and how much it matters; and write or review
publications and exhibits to check they are accurate. Public-sector
postings add advising others as the authoritative source, answering
queries, planning studies and judging the validity of sources.
Consulting historians work for agencies, companies, museums and media
producers, and are judged on deliverables on time. In games and other
media, historians research a setting's features and imagery and keep
story and play true to the period while the team takes creative
liberties.

The discipline's own statements describe the habits behind that work:
history is an interpretation built from surviving evidence; sources are
judged for credibility, position and perspective; conflicting evidence
improves the argument; and every claim leaves a trail others can follow.

This plugin holds that job for an agent on a product team: the method in
its skills, the steps that must happen in order in its workflows, and
nothing about any particular product. The project's profile names the
periods, places and people the product draws on.

## Why research drives design

A product set in the past makes claims about it whether it means to or
not. The cheapest time to get it right is before the art and writing are
made. That is why the role's main outputs are things makers can act on
at once: a brief that says what the answer means for each role, a
reference set artists can work from, and a fact check with fixes that
keep the work's intent. The roles that own design and art still decide;
the historian gives them evidence, options and constraints.

## Why evidence, interpretation and invention are kept apart

Products need invention: the record is silent on most of what a scene
shows. The harm is not invention but invention mistaken for history, by
the team or the audience. So every claim carries a grade (attested,
inferred, contested, unknown), and every deliberate departure goes in a
record with who chose it. Accuracy and authenticity can then both be
served: true where it matters, believable where the record is silent.

## Why the checks are independent

A researcher who misreads a source will misread it again when checking.
Each workflow therefore sends its checks (citations against sources,
items against holders' records, findings against evidence, pages
against the brief) to a reviewer who did not do the work. This matters
more now that generated text invents convincing citations: every
citation in this plugin's output must be one a second reader opened.

## Why Latin America first, and why it stays open

The plugin starts with Latin American history because that is where its
first work lies, and because the region tests the method hard: long
Indigenous histories recorded in painted books, inscriptions and memory,
then centuries of records made under colonial rule. Learning to read a
friar's account beside a community's own annals is the same skill needed
anywhere the record was written by outsiders. The skills are written so
that another region adds its own sources, archives and debates to the
same method.

## Why heritage ethics is part of the job

The peoples a product depicts have descendants who live today and whose
traditions are living culture. Professional codes ask historians to work
toward cultural inclusiveness and never to let advocacy distort the
record; international standards recognise Indigenous peoples' rights
over their cultural heritage and knowledge. In practice that means
checking every depiction for stereotype and erasure, leaving sacred and
restricted material out unless a community agrees, naming peoples as
they name themselves, and raising consultation with the owner early,
because only the owner can arrange it.

## Boundaries with neighbouring roles

| Role | Takes from the historian | Gives back |
|---|---|---|
| game-designer, writers | Briefs, timelines, glossaries, fact checks | Decisions, departures to record |
| technical-artist, graphic-designer | Reference sets and artist briefs | Original art for checking |
| sound-designer, ui-developer | Briefs on music, sound, writing and symbols | Work for checking |
| project-manager | Consultation needs, research packages | Priorities |
| The owner | Who to consult and why | Consultation, permissions, access |
