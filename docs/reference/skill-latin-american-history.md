# latin-american-history

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Skill |
| File | [`skills/latin-american-history/SKILL.md`](../../skills/latin-american-history/SKILL.md) |
| Loaded when | research concerns Latin America and the Caribbean in any period - Indigenous civilizations before European contact (Mesoamerica, the Andes, the Caribbean, Amazonia), contact and conquest, the Spanish and Portuguese colonial era, African diasporas, independence and the modern nations - to find the region's source types, archives and digital editions, its languages, its main debates, and the names and terms its peoples and scholars use. |
| Referenced by | [research-brief](workflow-research-brief.md), [reference-set](workflow-reference-set.md) |

## Inputs

A question about Latin America or the Caribbean, in any period.

## Outputs

The region's periods and peoples, source types, archives and digital editions, languages, names and debates that bear on it.

## At a glance

| Step | Does |
|---|---|
| Periods and peoples | Before contact, contact and conquest, colonial, independence, modern nations; whose period it is |
| Source types | Painted books, inscriptions, khipu, Indigenous-language texts, chronicles, administrative and legal records, archaeology |
| Where to look | National archives and libraries, the Archivo General de Indias, national institutes of anthropology and history, digital editions, the Handbook of Latin American Studies, journals and associations |
| Languages and names | Originals and scholarly editions; peoples named as they named themselves, with the common name noted |
| Debates | Whose account of conquest, colonial sources, violence and spectacle, population, mestizaje |
| Living communities | Descendants continue these cultures; apply heritage-ethics |

## Quality bar

- Periods and peoples are named precisely.
- Colonial sources are read as colonial sources, with community sources beside them.
- Holdings and identifiers are checked against the holder's catalogue.

## Documentation owed

A timeline and a glossary or naming guide for the product's period and place; an explanation of the debates a design choice depends on. Written in the same change as the work, one Diataxis kind per page.
