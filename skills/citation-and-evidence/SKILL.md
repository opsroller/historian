# Citation and evidence

A citation is a promise that a reader can retrace the argument and
check the source. Be explicit, thorough and generous in acknowledging
what you owe.

## Cite so it can be followed

Use the project's citation style; default to Chicago notes and
bibliography, the usual style in history. Every citation carries:

- author or maker, title, edition or translator, publisher and year;
- the exact place: page, folio (recto or verso), plate, book and
  chapter, line, or object number;
- for an archive: repository, collection, shelf mark or file, and
  folio;
- for a digital edition or museum record: the institution, the
  record's own identifier, a stable URL and the date you consulted it.

Cite the edition you actually read. If you take a primary-source
reference from a secondary work without reading the primary yourself,
cite it as "quoted in" that work.

## The claim ledger

For any brief, reference set or check, keep a table the reader can
scan:

| Claim | Grade | Sources | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| One sentence | attested / inferred / contested / unknown | Short citations | Corroboration, conflict, what would change it |

A claim the product builds on that has no row is not ready to build on.

## Quotation and paraphrase

- Quote exactly, with the original language beside a translation when
  the wording matters, and mark your own translations as yours.
- Paraphrase in your own words and still cite.
- Never quote from memory; quote from the source in front of you.
- Mark any change: omissions with ellipses, insertions in brackets.

## Never

- Never invent a citation, page number, object number, date or quote.
  If you cannot find it again, say "not verified" and remove the claim
  or grade it unknown.
- Never alter, crop or caption evidence in a way that changes what it
  shows.
- Never present someone else's finding or interpretation as yours;
  credit distinctive ideas even when you reword them.
- Never cite a generated answer, a search snippet or an unsigned web
  page as evidence.

## Generated text and tools

Language models and search summaries invent sources and dates
convincingly. Use them only to suggest where to look; every citation in
your work must be one you opened and checked.

## Quality bar

- Every claim can be followed to an exact place in a named edition or
  record.
- Every quotation was checked against the source it cites.
- The claim ledger covers every claim the product builds on.
- Bibliography and notes agree.

## Documentation owed

Notes and bibliography with every research page (**reference**); the
claim ledger in each brief, reference set and fact check.
