# History for design

A product is not a history book, but it makes claims about the past
whether it means to or not. The historian's job is to give the makers
the past at its richest, help them choose where to depart from it, and
make sure every departure is a choice.

## Accuracy and authenticity

- **Accuracy:** what the product shows matches the evidence.
- **Authenticity:** what the product feels like is true to the period,
  even where details are invented.

Products need both. Simplification is fine when it does not
misrepresent; invention is fine when it is a recorded choice. What is
not fine is an error nobody chose, or an invention presented as fact.

## Turning research into direction

For each brief, give the team:

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| The answer | What the evidence says, with its confidence |
| Must | Things that would be wrong to get wrong (they break the period, or misrepresent a people) |
| Opportunities | Rich, surprising, true details the team could use |
| Free to invent | Where the record is silent and the team may choose |
| Avoid | Common errors, anachronisms and stereotypes for this subject |
| Per role | What it means for design, art, writing, audio and UI |

Offer options, not verdicts: the owning role decides.

## The departures record

Keep one record per product of every deliberate departure:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| What the product does | One line |
| What history says | With citation and confidence |
| Why it departs | Play, clarity, scope, tone, or a gap in the record |
| Who decided and when | The role or person that chose it |
| How it is signalled | Credits, an in-product note, a site page, or not at all, and why |

A departure that touches how a living people is shown also passes
`heritage-ethics`.

## Checking what the team made

Read the design, script, art, audio, UI text or marketing and list
every historical claim, depiction and name it makes, explicit or
implied. For each, give a verdict:

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Supported | Matches the evidence |
| Departure (recorded) | Differs, and the departures record covers it |
| Error | Contradicts the evidence; give the fix |
| Anachronism | Belongs to another time or place; give what fits |
| Unsupported | No evidence either way; mark as invention or find evidence |
| Representation | Fails a `heritage-ethics` check |

Severity follows harm: misrepresenting a living people or a sacred
practice outranks a wrong date; an error in something the product
teaches outranks one in background detail.

## Shared references

Keep one of each for the product, used by every role:

- **Timeline** of the period, with the product's own fixed points.
- **Glossary and naming guide:** people, places, gods, titles, objects,
  with original and modern spellings, pronunciation where known, and
  the form the product uses.
- **Reference sets** per subject (`visual-and-material-reference`).

## Quality bar

- Every brief tells each role what it means for them.
- Every departure is in the record with who decided it.
- Every check lists claims with verdicts, evidence and fixes.
- Names are consistent with the glossary everywhere.

## Documentation owed

Briefs, the departures record, the timeline, the glossary and fact
checks as **reference**; the product's approach to the past as an
**explanation**.
