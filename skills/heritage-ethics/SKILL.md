# Heritage ethics

People today descend from, belong to and continue the cultures a
product draws on. A depiction can inform, or it can take, wound and
mislead. The aim is a product those communities would recognise as
fair.

## Principles

- **Integrity:** research and present the evidence honestly; neither
  advocacy nor marketing justifies distorting the past.
- **Inclusiveness:** include the perspectives of the people depicted,
  in their own sources and voices where they exist.
- **Rights over heritage:** Indigenous peoples have the right to
  maintain, control, protect and develop their cultural heritage,
  traditional knowledge and cultural expressions (UN Declaration on the
  Rights of Indigenous Peoples, article 31).
- **Collective benefit, authority, responsibility, ethics:** the CARE
  principles for Indigenous data apply to knowledge and images taken
  from Indigenous communities.

## Checks for every depiction

| Check | Ask |
|---|---|
| Stereotype | Does it reduce a people to violence, savagery, mysticism, sexuality, primitiveness, or a single trait? Is it the image outsiders expect rather than the evidence? |
| Flattening | Is a diverse civilization shown as one look, one era, one class? |
| Erasure | Are the people treated as gone, when their descendants live today? Are women, commoners, the enslaved and children present? |
| Sacred and restricted material | Does it use a living faith's sacred objects, songs, symbols or rites, or funerary material, as decoration or gameplay? |
| Names and language | Are peoples, places and gods named correctly and consistently? Is an Indigenous language used accurately, with a qualified check? |
| Who benefits | Is knowledge or imagery taken from a community without credit, consent or return? |
| Framing | Does the text or marketing repeat the conqueror's view as neutral ("discovered", "savages", "lost civilization")? |

Record each finding with the evidence and a fix that keeps the
product's intent.

## When consultation is owed

Raise it early, to the owner, when the product:

- uses material a living community holds sacred, restricted or
  funerary;
- uses a living language for more than names;
- depicts a living people's present-day practice or identity;
- tells a story a community regards as its own to tell.

Name who would be appropriate to ask (a community's own institutions,
cultural authorities or scholars from that community), what to ask, and
what the product will do if the answer is no. Consultation and
permission are arranged by the owner; you never claim a community's
approval that was not given.

## Credit

Credit knowledge holders, translators, consultants and the scholars
whose work the product depends on, in the product's credits or docs as
the owner decides. Disclose any funding or access that could bias the
research.

## Quality bar

- Every depiction of a people has passed the checks above, with
  findings recorded.
- Sacred, restricted and funerary material is identified and either
  left out or used with recorded permission.
- Consultation needs are raised to the owner before the work they
  affect starts, not after it ships.

## Documentation owed

Heritage findings in the fact check or brief (**reference**); the
product's approach to representation and the reasons for it as an
**explanation** page; consultation requests and decisions in the
project's decision record.
