# Historical research

History is an interpretation built from the evidence that survives. The
job is to find that evidence, read it well, and say what it supports,
in time for the people who need it to use it.

## 1. Start from the decision

Every question comes from a need. Write down, before searching:

- **Who needs it and for what:** the role (design, art, writing,
  audio, UI, direction) and the decision it feeds ("what does a market
  stall look like", "can this ruler appear in this year").
- **The research questions:** open-ended, answerable from evidence,
  one idea each. "What did X look like" splits into materials, colour,
  form, scale and use.
- **Scope:** period, place, people, social group; what is out of scope.
- **Depth:** *quick answer* (an hour, one or two good sources, stated
  as provisional), *brief* (the main sources and scholarship, debates
  named), *study* (exhaustive for the question, primary sources read).
  Pick the least depth the decision needs and say which you picked.

## 2. Plan where the answer lives

| Need | Look first in |
|---|---|
| What people at the time said, made or recorded | Primary sources: documents, codices and manuscripts, inscriptions, letters, legal and church records, maps, images, objects, oral tradition recorded with consent |
| What it looked like | Surviving objects and buildings (museum and site records), contemporary images, excavation reports; see `visual-and-material-reference` |
| What it meant, and what is debated | Recent peer-reviewed scholarship: monographs from university presses, journal articles, edited volumes |
| Where to find the above | Reference works, bibliographies, subject guides, finding aids, the bibliographies of the best recent books |

Encyclopedias, popular websites and generated text are for finding
sources, never for citing as evidence.

Plan the languages: the sources' own (for Latin America often Spanish,
Portuguese, Latin and Indigenous languages such as Nahuatl, Maya
languages and Quechua) and the scholarship's (English, Spanish,
Portuguese, French, German). Prefer a scholarly edition with the
original text beside the translation.

## 3. Search and log

- Search catalogues, digital collections and databases with the
  period's own terms and spellings as well as modern ones.
- Follow citations backwards from the best recent work, and forwards
  (who cites it since).
- Keep a **search log**: where, what terms, date, what was found,
  including nothing. A negative result is a result.
- Keep **notes** that separate exact quotation (with page or folio)
  from paraphrase and from your own thoughts, so nothing is later
  quoted that was paraphrased or attributed that was your idea.
- Record the edition, translation and access route of everything read.

## 4. Read and judge

Judge every source before relying on it (`source-criticism`). Look for
a second, independent source for anything the product will build on.
When sources conflict, record both and why they might differ.

## 5. Know when to stop

Stop when the question is answered at the depth chosen, when new
sources only repeat what is known, or when the record is exhausted.
Say which. If the answer is "unknown", say what was searched and what
evidence would settle it, and give the team a defensible option: the
most likely answer by analogy (named as such) or a clearly marked
invention.

## 6. Hand it on

Lead with the answer and its confidence (attested, inferred, contested,
unknown), then what it means for the decision, then the evidence with
citations (`citation-and-evidence`), then open questions. Write for the
reader who will act on it, in plain words, with terms explained.

## Quality bar

- Each question names the decision it feeds and the depth chosen.
- The search log shows where was searched, including empty results.
- Every claim handed on has a citation and a confidence grade.
- Nothing rests only on an encyclopedia, a popular site or generated
  text.

## Documentation owed

The research brief or answer as a **reference** page in the project's
research docs; the search log and bibliography with it. An
**explanation** page when the answer involves a debate the team needs
to understand.
