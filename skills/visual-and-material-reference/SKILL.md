# Visual and material reference

Artists build what they can see. A reference set turns research into
images and facts they can work from, each one traceable and honest
about how much it shows.

## What a reference set covers

Choose the categories the product needs, for example:

| Category | Typical evidence |
|---|---|
| Architecture and space | Excavated and standing buildings, site plans, reconstructions (labelled), contemporary depictions |
| Objects | Museum objects with catalogue records; excavation finds |
| Dress, adornment and bodies | Surviving textiles and jewellery, contemporary images, written descriptions |
| Materials and techniques | Stone, wood, metal, featherwork, ceramics, textiles, pigments: how they were made, worked and finished |
| Colour | Surviving pigment, conservation and scientific analysis; note fading, restoration and later repainting |
| Iconography, writing and ornament | Motifs, glyphs, symbols and what they meant; who could use them |
| Landscape, plants and animals | Environmental history, period maps, depictions, present-day survivals |
| Sound and music | Surviving instruments, depictions of performance, descriptions; reconstructions labelled as such |

## For every item, record

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| What | Object, building or image, in one line |
| Holder and identifier | Museum, library, archive or site; catalogue, accession or folio number; stable URL |
| Date and place | Of making, with the holder's confidence |
| Kind of evidence | Surviving original, contemporary depiction, later depiction, modern reconstruction, replica |
| Shows / does not show | What an artist may take from it, and what it cannot tell them (colour lost, a later restoration, an elite object not an everyday one) |
| Rights | Public domain or open licence (name it, e.g. CC0) with the page that says so; otherwise "reference only, not for reuse" |
| Sensitivity | Sacred, funerary or restricted for a living community? See `heritage-ethics` |

Use the holder's own record, not a reposted copy. Open-access
collections that mark images as public domain or CC0 are the safest
source of images to place in the project; anything else stays a link.

## Evidence versus reconstruction

- Label every reconstruction, painting or render as modern and name
  who made it and on what evidence.
- Remember survival bias: stone and gold survive, wood, feathers, paint
  and cloth mostly do not. Fill the gaps with evidence-based inference
  and say so.
- Distinguish elite, ritual and everyday material; a product's world
  needs all three.
- Note regional and period differences; a motif from one city or
  century does not stand for a whole civilization.

## Brief the artists

- Lead with the essentials: forms, materials, palette, proportions,
  wear, and the few things that would be wrong.
- Give do and avoid lists, each tied to evidence.
- Say plainly: reference informs original work. Do not trace, copy or
  photobash reference images into shipped art; the project's
  `provenance` rules apply to anything shipped.

## Quality bar

- Every item has holder, identifier, date, place, kind of evidence and
  rights status.
- Every reconstruction is labelled as one.
- Every category the product needs has at least one surviving-evidence
  item or an explicit "no surviving evidence; inferred from ...".
- No item marked sensitive is in the set without the decision recorded.

## Documentation owed

The reference set as a **reference** page (one per subject, linked
from the project's art-direction or design docs); an **explanation**
page for any interpretive choice the art direction rests on.
